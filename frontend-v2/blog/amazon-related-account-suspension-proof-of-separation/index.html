Amazon related account suspension: proving two accounts are genuinely separate

Amazon flagged an overlap between your account and another one. The appeal that works does not deny the overlap exists, it proves the two businesses are genuinely run apart despite it.

Likith, Head of Operations · Published September 13, 2026 · 6 min read

A related account suspension notice means Amazon's system detected a specific technical overlap, a shared device, network, payment method, or personal detail, between your account and another registered seller account. The appeal that actually works names that overlap and proves separation, rather than denying a connection Amazon's system has already found. The rule against undisclosed multiple accounts sits inside Amazon's own Seller Code of Conduct.

Quick answer

Proof of separation means identifying the specific overlap Amazon flagged, then documenting that each account uses distinct ownership, payment methods, and operational infrastructure going forward, even if a shared element existed in the past.

What triggers a related account link

Amazon's system checks for overlapping signals across all registered seller accounts, including a shared home network or IP address, a bank account or card linked to more than one account, the same device used to access two different Seller Central logins, or matching business details such as an address, phone number, or tax ID. Any single overlap can be enough to trigger a flag, even without any intention to operate two connected accounts.

Evidence that proves separation, not denial

  • Distinct ownership documentation, business registration, tax ID, and identity verification specific to your account alone.
  • A separated payment method, a new card or bank account replacing any shared one, with documentation showing the change.
  • Independent operational infrastructure, a different device and network used to access your account going forward.
  • A specific, honest explanation of the relationship behind the overlap, rather than a denial that any connection exists.

Shared devices and networks are the hardest overlap to fully separate

A shared card can be replaced in a day. A shared home network or a family computer used for both accounts takes more deliberate separation, a distinct device, a distinct connection, and evidence of the change, before an appeal citing this fix carries real weight.

Writing the appeal around the specific overlap

An appeal that opens with "I have never operated another account" without naming what Amazon's system actually detected reads as unresolved, since it does not answer the specific signal behind the suspension. An appeal that opens by naming the overlap directly, a family member's account sharing your home network, for example, and then documents the separation, answers the actual question a reviewer is checking.

If the accounts really were operated together

If two accounts were genuinely run by the same person or business without Amazon's prior authorization, the honest path is disclosing this directly and requesting guidance on which account to retain, rather than continuing to deny a connection that is accurate. Amazon does allow related accounts with prior written permission in specific circumstances, which is a separate process from an appeal after the fact. Building a proof of separation case, or evaluating whether disclosure is the better path for your specific situation, is exactly what Appeal Edge's suspension appeal service helps sellers work through.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to close one of the accounts?

Not necessarily. If the accounts are genuinely separate businesses, proving that separation is the goal, not closing either one, unless Amazon's decision specifically requires it.

How long does separating a shared payment method take to show as resolved?

Replacing the payment method itself is immediate, but include documentation of the change and the date in your appeal so the reviewer can verify it directly.

What if I genuinely do not know what triggered the flag?

Review your own account history for any shared device, network, payment method, or personal detail with another seller you know, since the notice does not always specify which signal was detected.

Can family members legally run separate Amazon accounts?

Yes, but only with Amazon's prior written permission for a related account, and each account still needs to operate independently to avoid a future flag.
