Inauthentic

Got an inauthentic complaint for a unit you never touched? Check commingled inventory.

Stickerless commingled inventory means your FBA units and another seller's units sit in the same bin under the same ASIN. When one of them turns out to be fake or mislabeled, the complaint doesn't know whose unit it was, so it lands on every seller on that listing.

Khushi Narwal, COO · Published July 26, 2026 · Updated August 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Stickerless commingled inventory is one of the least understood risks in FBA, mainly because it produces suspensions that feel completely unfair. A seller who sourced every unit correctly can still receive an inauthentic complaint traced to a unit they never touched, because the inventory system itself pooled their stock with someone else's. These complaints are still evaluated under Amazon's Intellectual Property Policy, whether or not the seller's own sourcing was at fault.

Quick answer

If your inauthentic complaint names a unit that does not match your own inventory records, check whether that ASIN uses stickerless commingled inventory. Amazon pools units from multiple sellers under one ASIN, so one bad unit from another seller can trigger a complaint against everyone active on that listing, including you.

What stickerless commingled inventory means

When you send inventory into FBA without individual unit labeling, Amazon can store it as stickerless inventory, meaning your units and other sellers' units of the identical product get pooled together in the same storage location under the shared ASIN. When an order ships, Amazon fulfills it from whichever unit is physically closest, not specifically from your batch. This is the default setup for many product categories unless a seller actively opts into individual labeling.

How a complaint becomes yours

If even one unit in that shared pool is counterfeit, mislabeled, or otherwise problematic, and a buyer reports it, or a brand owner conducts a test purchase and catches it, Amazon frequently cannot determine which specific seller's unit actually reached that customer. Rather than leave the issue unresolved, the response often extends to every seller currently active on that shared ASIN, regardless of whether their own sourcing was entirely legitimate.

How to opt out

During the inbound shipment planning process in Seller Central, you can choose individual unit labeling, sometimes referred to as the Amazon barcode requirement, which assigns each of your units its own FNSKU barcode. This keeps every unit traceable specifically to your account rather than pooled anonymously with other sellers. There is typically a per unit labeling cost associated with this choice, which is the tradeoff against the commingling exposure it removes.

How to prove it in an appeal

Being commingled does not remove the need to document your own sourcing. An appeal still needs your invoices and chain of custody records covering the relevant date range, showing your units came from a legitimate source. Where available, request any specific order or complaint details Amazon has provided, since narrowing the timeframe can help establish whether the complaint aligns with a period your own inventory was actually in that pool. Pair that documentation with a clear statement of your labeling decision going forward.

Commingling explains the risk, it doesn't excuse the appeal

Raising commingled inventory as a contributing factor is reasonable, but an appeal built entirely around "it wasn't my unit" without documentation of your own sourcing tends to read as an excuse rather than an explanation. Bring both the context and the proof.

Should every seller pay for labeled inventory

Not necessarily. For lower value, high volume commodity products, the labeling cost can outweigh the risk for many sellers. For higher value goods, or for brand owners and authorized resellers where an inauthentic complaint carries outsized consequences relative to the labeling fee, individual labeling is generally worth the added cost.

Frequently asked questions

What is FBA commingled inventory?

A stickerless FBA setup where your units and other sellers' units of the same product share the same storage bin under one shared ASIN, rather than each seller's units being individually labeled and tracked.

Can I be suspended for a fake product I never sold?

Yes, if your listing is commingled and a counterfeit or mislabeled unit from another seller reaches a customer, Amazon often cannot trace it to a specific seller and can act against everyone sharing that ASIN.

How do I stop my inventory from being commingled?

Enroll your units in FNSKU labeling during the inbound shipment process, which requires each unit to carry a barcode specific to your account, keeping it traceable to you specifically.

What documentation helps in an appeal caused by commingled inventory?

Your own sourcing invoices and chain of custody records for the relevant date range, and any specific order or complaint details Amazon has provided, alongside a stated decision on labeling going forward.
