Should you open a new Amazon account instead of appealing? Why that ends worse

A new account feels faster than fighting the suspension. In practice it is one of the most reliable ways to turn a fixable case into a genuinely unfixable one.

Likith, Head of Operations · Published August 14, 2026 · 7 min read

When a suspension notice arrives, opening a new account under a different name, a family member's details, or a new email feels like the fast option. Appealing feels slow and uncertain by comparison. The problem is that Amazon's systems are specifically built to catch this exact move, and getting caught converts one problem into two. Operating a second account without disclosure is itself a separate violation of Amazon's Seller Code of Conduct.

Quick answer

Opening a new Amazon account while another account is suspended is very likely to be detected through shared device, browser, payment method, address, or identity signals, and it converts a single fixable suspension into a related account violation, which is significantly harder to resolve. The faster path is almost always appealing the original account properly, even though it does not feel that way in the moment.

How Amazon actually links accounts

Account linking is not based on a single signal. Amazon cross references dozens of data points: device fingerprints, browser characteristics, IP addresses and network patterns, payment methods, bank account details, business registration numbers, phone numbers, and shipping or return addresses. A new account set up on the same laptop, using the same bank account for payouts, or shipping returns to the same warehouse address as a suspended account, carries a real risk of being connected, even if the seller name, business name, and email are all different.

What happens when a linked account is found

When Amazon connects a new account to a suspended one, the result is typically a related account suspension on the new account, and it can also complicate or close off the appeal path on the original account, since the related account activity becomes part of the case against it. What started as one solvable issue, tied to a specific document, order, or process problem, becomes a related account violation, which requires proving there was no intent to circumvent the suspension, a fundamentally harder case to make. This guide covers what a related account notice actually means and how it is appealed.

Family members and business partners are not a safe workaround

Opening an account under a spouse's, sibling's, or business partner's name does not remove the risk if any device, address, payment method, or piece of identifying information overlaps with the suspended account. Shared households and shared business operations are exactly the pattern this detection is designed to catch.

Why appealing is actually the faster path, even though it does not feel like it

A well built appeal on a first suspension, when the root cause is identified correctly and the plan of action is specific, often resolves within the review windows Amazon publishes for the relevant case type. A related account violation, once triggered, tends to take considerably longer and has a real chance of failing outright, since it requires proving a negative, that there was no intent to evade the original suspension, which is inherently harder to document than resolving a specific, named issue. The seemingly slower path of appealing correctly the first time is, in the typical case, actually the faster route to being back on the marketplace.

A typical situation this applies to

A common pattern: a seller gets an inauthentic complaint suspension, panics at the thought of losing momentum during a busy season, and opens a second account the same week to keep selling while the first case is reviewed. Weeks later, the second account is suspended for a related account violation, and the original appeal is now complicated by the new account's existence. In almost every version of this situation, the seller would have been selling again sooner by putting that same energy into a properly built plan of action on the original account.

If you have already opened a second account

If this has already happened, the honest path forward is addressing both accounts directly rather than hoping the connection goes unnoticed. A plan of action that acknowledges the second account, explains the circumstances, and demonstrates the accounts are being consolidated or the second one closed, is a stronger position than continuing to operate as though the connection does not exist. Frequently asked questions

Can Amazon tell if I open a new account after being suspended?

Very often, yes. Amazon cross references device, browser, IP, payment method, bank account, and address details, and a new account sharing any of these with a suspended account carries a real risk of being linked.

What happens if my new account gets linked to my suspended one?

The new account typically receives a related account suspension, and the activity can complicate the appeal on the original account as well, since it becomes part of the case being reviewed.

Is it safe to open a new account under a family member's name?

Not automatically. If any device, payment method, address, or other identifying detail overlaps with the suspended account, the accounts can still be linked regardless of whose name is on the new one.

Is appealing really faster than starting over?

In most cases, yes. A properly built appeal on the original issue tends to resolve within Amazon's published review windows, while a related account violation triggered by a second account typically takes longer and is harder to win.
