Amazon not responding to your appeal? here's the escalation ladder.

Silence after a second appeal isn't a dead end, it's a signal to change channel, not content. Here's the escalation path that actually reaches a reviewer, and when resubmitting the same plan stops helping.

Apeksha Namdev, CEO · Published July 26, 2026 · Updated August 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Silence after a second appeal feels like the end of the road, but it usually means the channel needs to change, not the tone of the message. Here's the actual escalation path inside Seller Central that reaches a reviewer, and the point where resubmitting the same plan stops being useful. Escalating does not change what is being evaluated. It is still measured against Amazon's Seller Code of Conduct.

Quick answer

To escalate a stalled Amazon appeal, stop resubmitting the same plan and open a case with Account Health Support instead. That routes the case to a specialist who can actually review it, rather than back into the same automated queue.

Why resubmitting isn't the next move

If an appeal has already been rejected twice with no substantive explanation, submitting a fourth version that's nearly identical to the third rarely produces a different outcome. Before doing anything else, reread the rejection language closely for any specific gap it names, even a small one, since that's the strongest signal available for what to actually change.

Step one: open an Account Health Support case

This is a distinct channel from the appeal submission itself. Inside Seller Central, open a case directly with Account Health Support, reference your existing case ID, and ask a specific question rather than a general request to review again. Something like naming exactly what section of your plan you'd like feedback on tends to get a more substantive reply than a broad "please reconsider."

Step two: request specialist routing

Some suspension types route to specialized review teams. If Account Health Support keeps returning generic responses after a genuinely specific question, explicitly ask for the case to be routed to a specialist team for that violation type. This request alone doesn't guarantee reassignment, but it's the mechanism available, and it's underused.

Step three: a documented compliance angle, if relevant

For accounts with significant revenue at stake and a genuinely strong compliance record, referencing documented regulatory correspondence can sometimes support a final escalation. This step is rarely necessary before the first two are fully exhausted, and it should never be the opening move.

When to stop escalating and rewrite the plan instead

If an escalation attempt finally produces a response that names something specific, whether it's a missing document type or an unclear root cause, that's the signal to stop escalating and rewrite the plan around that exact gap. The goal of escalation was never to win through persistence, it was to get specific enough feedback to write a plan that actually resolves the issue.

Two rejections doesn't mean the door is closed

Being rejected twice doesn't permanently disqualify an account from further appeals. Most suspension types accept continued appeals as long as each one reflects a genuinely new finding, not the same content resubmitted with different wording.

Frequently asked questions

What does it mean when Amazon isn't responding to my appeal at all?

Usually a review queue delay, or the resubmission wasn't substantively different enough from a prior version to route past initial screening.

How do I escalate an Amazon appeal that keeps getting rejected?

Open a case through Account Health Support referencing your existing case ID, ask a specific question about what's missing, and request routing to a specialist review team if the response stays generic.

Is there a limit to how many times I can appeal?

There's no official cap, but resubmitting genuinely identical content is the most common way sellers waste attempts without changing the outcome.

When should I stop appealing on my own and get help?

After two rejections that gave no specific, actionable feedback, since further resubmission without a real new root cause finding rarely changes the result.
