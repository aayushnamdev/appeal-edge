Amazon trademark violation appeal: the exact steps in seller central.

Most guidance about IP complaints focuses on strategy. This is the mechanical part: where to click in Account Health, what the appeal form actually asks for, and the retraction path that can end the complaint at its source.

Likith, Head of Operations · Published July 26, 2026 · Updated September 13, 2026 · 6 min read

A trademark or IP violation notice tells you a listing was removed and a complaint is on file, but it rarely explains where to click next. Here is the mechanical path: where the appeal option actually lives, what the form fields expect, and the retraction route that can resolve the complaint faster than an appeal alone. The underlying rules sellers are held to are published in Amazon's Intellectual Property Policy.

Quick answer

There is no separate appeals page. Open Account Health, go to Policy Compliance or the Violations tab, find the row for the affected ASIN, and the Appeal or edit option sits directly on that row.

Where the edit button is in Account Health

The appeal option for a trademark or IP violation lives on the violation row itself, inside Account Health under Policy Compliance or Violations. It's usually labeled Appeal, or reachable through a small edit icon next to the listing status on that same row.

Finding the violation in Account Health

Open Account Health from the main Seller Central menu. Under Policy Compliance, or the Violations tab depending on your account layout, you'll see a list of active issues. The row for a trademark or IP complaint shows the affected ASIN and the type of violation. The appeal option sits directly on that row, usually labeled Appeal or accessible through an edit icon next to the listing status.

Open the full notice text before starting the appeal. Some notices include the rights owner's name and a contact method, either directly in the message or referenced as available through Brand Registry's Report Infringement tool if the complaint originated there.

What the appeal form fields actually expect

  1. Root cause explanation. State specifically how the product was sourced and why you believed the use was authorized, not a general statement that you didn't intend to infringe.
  2. Corrective actions. What you've already done: pulled the listing, contacted your supplier, removed the disputed branding from packaging or images if applicable.
  3. Supporting documents. Invoices from an authorized distributor, a license or authorization letter if one exists, and clear images of the product and its packaging as received.
  4. Retraction attachment. If the rights owner has agreed to retract the complaint, attach that confirmation directly, since it carries significant weight with the review team.

The retraction route through the rights owner

Many trademark and IP complaints originate through Amazon's Report Infringement tool or Brand Registry, meaning a real rights owner filed it, not an automated system. If you can identify that rights owner from the notice, a direct and professional request explaining your authorization or sourcing, with documentation attached, can lead them to submit a retraction to Amazon. A retraction closes the violation at its source and typically moves faster than waiting on a standard appeal review. For exactly what that request needs to say, see Amazon IP complaint retraction letter: what actually gets a rights owner to withdraw. Identifying the right contact and writing that request is exactly what Appeal Edge's IP complaint removal service handles for trademark, copyright, and counterfeit complaints.

Removing the listing doesn't close the violation

Deleting or deactivating the flagged listing does not resolve the violation on your account record. Amazon still expects a completed appeal, or a retraction, to close it out. Leaving it unresolved keeps the strike active against your account health.

Common reasons this appeal gets rejected

  • No documentation connecting the product to a legitimate, authorized source
  • A corrective action section that repeats the root cause instead of describing a completed step
  • Resubmitting the same appeal content Amazon already reviewed and rejected, without addressing the specific gap named in the rejection
  • No preventive step describing how future sourcing will avoid the same issue

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the appeal button for a trademark violation in seller central?

Open Account Health, go to the Policy Compliance or Violations section, find the specific listing row for the violation, and the appeal option sits directly on that row.

What if the notice doesn't name the rights owner?

Some performance notifications include limited contact information. Check the full notice text in your case log, and if none is listed, the appeal itself is your primary path rather than a direct retraction.

Can I get the complaint retracted instead of appealing?

Yes, if the rights owner agrees your use was authorized or the complaint was made in error, they can retract it directly with Amazon, which typically resolves faster than a standard appeal.

What documents does Amazon expect for a trademark violation appeal?

Proof of authorization or license to use the brand, invoices showing legitimate sourcing, and images of the product and packaging showing the actual branding as received.
